Techniques

How we work with glass

What we do every day in the workshop, explained without jargon. If you are not sure which glass suits you, ask us.

Cutting

Cutting glass sheets

Everything starts with cutting the sheet to size. For hand cutting we use a diamond cutter, a square, a tape measure and special pliers to snap the glass along the scored line.

Decoration

Sandblasting

A vinyl stencil with the design is stuck to the glass and sand is blasted at it under pressure. The exposed parts turn frosted and the covered parts stay clear. It works for floral patterns, logos, text or any drawing.

Finishes

Bevelling and edge polishing

Bevelling cuts the edge at an angle and gives it depth; it is common on mirrors. Polishing smooths the edge so it does not cut and looks clean.

Finishes

Frosting and etching

With sand or acid we leave frosted areas next to clear ones. Acid etching is also used to mark text, logos or the stamp that certifies a glass.

Doors

Glazing doors

  1. We check the door and prepare it.
  2. We fix the bottom bead with nails and silicone.
  3. We set the glass in the opening.
  4. We seal the edge with silicone.
  5. We close it with the top bead.

On fire doors the fire-resistant strips go in first, and at the end a wax the colour of the wood hides the nail holes.

Materials

Types of glass

Laminated: several layers bonded with a plastic film. If it breaks, the pieces stay stuck together.
Tempered: heat-treated to make it stronger. If it breaks, it goes into small pieces that do not cut.
Fire-resistant: withstands high temperatures and slows the spread of fire.

Heritage

Lathe cutting

Cutting glass by hand is the craft where it all began. Rafa Osuna cut glass in Madrid before opening his workshop in Villacañas in 1980, and his lathes are still in the building.

It is entirely handwork: a diamond wheel wears the glass away little by little while the cutter controls pressure, angle and speed. We no longer take cut-glass orders; we tell it here because it is where we come from.
